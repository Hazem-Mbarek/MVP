## 1.2 Company Description

NordRoute Logistics GmbH is a fictional German logistics and moving company headquartered in Dortmund. The company provides road transportation, international moving, warehousing, customs and documentation assistance, and related logistics services.

The company's primary operating market is Germany, with regular international road transport services connecting Germany with France, Belgium, the Netherlands and Poland.

NordRoute focuses on small and medium-sized commercial shipments, full truckload transportation, less-than-truckload transportation, international household moves and business relocations.

The company operates its own transport fleet and warehouse while also using selected external transport partners when additional capacity or specialised equipment is required.

## 1.3 Business Positioning

NordRoute positions itself as a mid-sized logistics operator offering a combination of:

* direct customer communication;
* road freight transportation;
* international moving services;
* shipment coordination;
* customs and documentation assistance;
* short-term and medium-term storage;
* cross-docking;
* claims coordination.

The company primarily competes on reliability, communication, flexible scheduling and its ability to coordinate transportation and related services through a single operational contact.

# 4. Service Portfolio

NordRoute provides full truckload road transportation for customers requiring an entire vehicle or substantial vehicle capacity.

Typical FTL shipments include:

* palletised commercial goods;
* manufacturing components;
* retail goods;
* machinery;
* household relocation cargo;
* consolidated business shipments.

FTL services can be arranged across the company's primary Germany–France, Germany–Belgium, Germany–Netherlands and Germany–Poland corridors.

## 4.2 Less Than Truckload (LTL)

LTL services are designed for shipments that do not require an entire truck.

Typical LTL shipments include:

* individual pallets;
* several pallets;
* smaller commercial consignments;
* partial loads.

LTL shipments may be consolidated with other compatible shipments.

The final price and transit arrangement depend on:

* shipment dimensions;
* weight;
* number of pallets;
* origin;
* destination;
* pickup requirements;
* delivery requirements;
* service level.

Groupage may reduce transportation costs but can involve additional handling and a longer transit time compared with dedicated FTL transportation.

# 8. Important Data Boundary

The company profile contains **relatively stable company knowledge**.

Examples include:

* warehouse locations;
* warehouse IDs;
* approximate warehouse capacity;
* loading-dock count;
* forklift availability as a company resource;
* standard warehouse services;
* storage limitations;
* standard operating hours;
* geographic role of each warehouse.

The operational database should contain **dynamic information**, such as:

* current warehouse occupancy;
* currently available pallet capacity;
* current dock availability;
* incoming warehouse shipments;
* outbound warehouse shipments;
* goods currently stored;
* warehouse reservations;
* current inter-warehouse transfers;
* transfer schedules;
* current vehicle availability.

The AI system should not infer dynamic operational status from this document.

For example:

**Knowledge/RAG question:**

> "How many pallets can the Leipzig warehouse physically hold?"

→ Approximately 300 pallets.

**Database question:**

> "How many pallets can we store in Leipzig today?"

→ Query the current warehouse capacity/occupancy data.

**Combined question:**

> "Can we accept 120 pallets into Leipzig tomorrow?"

→ The agent should combine company knowledge with current database information, including available capacity, reservations, expected arrivals and operational constraints.

The same principle applies to Dortmund.
